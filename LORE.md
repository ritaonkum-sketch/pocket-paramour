## 1 · THE WORLD

### 1.1 The Kingdoms

The story takes place across three kingdoms whose histories are entangled:

- **Aethermoor** — the surviving kingdom. Ruled (de facto for six centuries) by
  Queen Aenor. Aenor's grandson is Crown Prince Caspian, current heir.
  Aethermoor is built **above the ground that holds The Deep** (see §1.2).
- **Nocthera** — the **fallen** kingdom. Once a peer of Aethermoor across the
  northern sea. Collapsed roughly 600 years ago, one generation after its heir
  was sealed away. The land is bone now. (The land has been seen breathing
  again in Noir's deepest care route — see §6.6.)
- **Caerwyn** — the **destroyed** kingdom. The heroine's birth kingdom, far
  from Aethermoor. **Destroyed by Aenor ~150 years ago in her hunt for the
  hidden Weaver child** (the heroine herself). Now ruins. The kingdom the
  heroine may, in one route, return to and rebuild (see §10.5).

The three kingdoms were rivals before the events that broke Nocthera and
Caerwyn. They were trade partners, marriage partners, and (via Aethermoor's
import-marriage tradition) Weaver-source kingdoms.

### 1.2 The Deep — and the bond-magic that makes the world livable

Beneath Aethermoor lies **THE DEEP** — a vein of raw, pure magic that runs
through the underground stone. The Deep is older than the kingdom built on
top of it. It cannot be used directly by ordinary people; it is too wild, too
pure. **It is the magical bones of the world.**

The Deep can only be *read and woven* by **Soul Weavers** — rare individuals
who can take the raw underground magic and *weave it into bonds between
people.* The bonds are the bond-magic; the bond-magic is the surface-usable
form. Together they form what holds the kingdom together: streets exist
because someone trusted a builder; walls stand because someone protected a
city; magic flows because someone loved someone. **Weaving is the work that
translates The Deep into a livable world.**

- When bonds are strong, the kingdom is whole.
- When bonds thin, the world thins.
- When The Deep is unwoven, it leaks up — **as dark magic, as wounds.**
  (See §4.)

### 1.3 Weavers are not born in Aethermoor

**Weavers can only be found outside Aethermoor.** They are never born within
its borders. The Aethermoor lineage carries the diluted royal bond-magic —
not the Weaver gift. For centuries, Aethermoor has *imported* Weavers from
other kingdoms via political marriage. This is the kingdom's deepest
structural mechanism, and its deepest vulnerability.

**Exception:** see §6.7 — **Proto**, born in Aethermoor, the rare Weaver
whose existence the kingdom should not have produced. The mystery of how is
an open canon question (see §8.7).

### 1.4 The Weaver-Marriage Law

By the law of Aethermoor, **marrying a Weaver gives the spouse a legitimate
claim to the throne.** This single statute is the engine of much of the
kingdom's history.

- Marrying a Weaver = legitimacy.
- Marrying a Weaver = threat to whoever currently holds the throne.
- Marrying a Weaver = political power.

This is why Aethermoor's nobility compete fiercely for Weaver-marriages;
this is why imported Weavers are the kingdom's most contested figures; this
is why **Aenor must control, marry, or consume any Weaver who enters her
kingdom.** Selene, Veyra, and the heroine were all brought into Aethermoor
under this law (or its shadow).

### 1.5 Why the kingdom is collapsing now

For 150 years, Aenor has consumed every Weaver who entered Aethermoor. The
import pipeline has dried up — the other kingdoms have run out of Weavers
willing to be brought to Aethermoor, and Aenor's hunger has continued
anyway. **The Deep is no longer being woven.** It leaks up as wounds. The
eastern wood is sick. Towns past the headland are bones. The wards are
thinning. The kingdom is becoming what was beneath it: raw, unwoven, ruined.

## 2 · THE WEAVERS (1–7)

There have been seven Soul Weavers in the kingdom's history. Aenor's reign
overlaps with all of them.

### 2.1 SELENE · the First Weaver

**Status: consumed by Aenor by accident ~650 years ago. Name erased from
every kingdom record. Remembered only by Aenor herself, in private.**

Selene was brought to Aethermoor from another kingdom to **marry Aenor's
older brother AELRIC**, the original heir of the throne (see §3.1, §7). It
was a standard political Weaver-marriage under the Weaver-Marriage Law
(§1.4). She was meant to shore up Aethermoor's bloodline.

During the courtship period, **Selene and Aenor fell in love.** Aenor was
queer (§3.1) — her preference for women was the kind of preference Aethermoor
court culture had no language for. The love was unspoken and real. It was the
first deep love either young woman had had.

**Aelric grew jealous.** He could not openly accuse Selene of preferring his
sister — the love had no court-language to accuse — but he felt it, and he
was frightened of losing his bride to her. **He attempted an "accident" to
kill Aenor.** *(The exact mechanism — poisoned wine? a tampered staircase?
a hunting trap? — is a deliberate mystery slot future writers may flesh out.)*

Selene saw the danger and **threw herself between Aenor and death.** Aenor,
young and untrained and in lethal panic, her wild royal bond-magic lashed
out and **accidentally consumed Selene's bond-light to save herself.** Selene
died in Aenor's arms in under a minute.

**What it gave Aenor.** Vitality she had never felt. Her modest royal magic,
previously a candle, became a furnace. For one heartbeat she felt invincible.
Then she understood what the magic had done.

**What Aenor did next.** She wept over Selene's body for three days. She had
Selene buried beneath the cathedral with full state honors. She **had every
record of Selene's name burned** — not to hide the crime, but because she
could not bear to read it. She kept one thing: a ring Selene had given her
at sixteen. **She has worn it for six hundred years.** And she **swore she
would never do it again.**

**Aelric's fate.** Knowing what he had caused, terrified of his sister's
new power, **Aelric stepped aside.** He abdicated. Aenor became Queen.
(Aelric's fate after the abdication — exile, recluse, faded from records,
died young — is an open canon slot, see §8.1.)

She broke the never-again vow fifty years later with Veyra (§2.2). The Aenor
pattern was born from a broken promise to a friend — and a love.

**Future-arc hook (§10):** Selene's name has not been spoken aloud in
Aethermoor in six centuries. Aenor still whispers it in her sleep. The
moment — wherever it lands in the story — when Aenor finally says
*"Selene"* aloud to another living person belongs to whichever character
earns it. It is the single most important word in her vocabulary.

### 2.2 Veyra · the Second Weaver

**Status: consumed by Aenor on the spot in a magical duel with Corvin,
~600 years ago. Buried by Elian at the rowan.**

Veyra was a noble of **Nocthera**. As a young teenager she had slipped from
Nocthera into the **Thornwood** to meet **Elian** in secret. Their love was
true but forbidden — she was Nocthera royalty, he was a wood-warden; the
love could not be made public. She was **Elian's first love.** (See §6.5.)

Years later, Veyra was betrothed in political marriage to Prince Corvin
Noctalis, heir of Nocthera. The engagement became real love. She chose
Corvin.

Then **Cassien** entered the picture (§8.5). A Duke of Aethermoor from a
cadet branch of the royal house — a man with royal blood close enough to
mount a throne-claim under the Weaver-Marriage Law (§1.4). Cassien sought
to marry Veyra. **If he succeeded, he could legally overthrow Aenor.**

Cassien brought Veyra to Aethermoor. **Aenor met her too — and tried to
convince Veyra to "join hands" with her.** Aenor wanted Veyra as her own
Weaver-partner. *The offer was queer-coded, the way her love for Selene had
been.* Veyra refused both Cassien and Aenor. She wanted Corvin.

**Corvin came to Aethermoor to protect her.** He and Aenor **fought with
magic.** Catastrophic.

To win the duel, Aenor **consumed Veyra's bond-light on the spot.** She
gained Veyra's power. She defeated Corvin and **sealed him** (see §6.6).
Veyra's body fell — drained but intact.

**Cassien — witnessing all of it, *calculating* —** saw that Aenor was now
the strongest piece on the board. He proposed an **alliance:** he would
marry her, bring his cadet-branch royal legitimacy and military strength,
and reign at her side. **Aenor accepted.** They shared a bed because the
alliance required it; they never had a child naturally. (See §8.5 for
Cassien's arc and his eventual exhumation.)

**Elian, in the Thornwood, felt Veyra's consumption via the forest-whisper.**
He followed it. Found her body where it had fallen. *No one else had come
for her.* He carried her to the rowan in his wood and buried her — the
first stone (§6.5).

*(Proto recollection — Ch25 canon: Proto did not meet Veyra in life. He was
born ~150 years ago, four centuries after Veyra was already gone. Veyra's
last words were echoed in the wardwork of Corvin's seal; when Proto chose
the wardwork over dying ~150 years ago, those echoes found him. He carries
them now, and delivers them at the two-stones clearing in Ch25.)*

### 2.3 The Third Weaver

**Status: sealed her own daughter; the act broke her mind. Walked the inland
road and was lost to the wood.**

The Third Weaver tried to protect her own daughter from Aenor by sealing the
daughter's magic away. The act broke her mind. The mage tradition records
her as *"walked the inland road and was lost to the wood."* Whether Aenor
ever finished her, or whether the wood took her, is unspecified.

### 2.4 WREN · the Fourth Weaver

**Status: Elian's second buried love. Sole survivor of her village and
her kingdom (both burned by Aenor). Killed by Aenor at thirty, after
fleeing.**

Wren was born in a village of another kingdom *(both kingdom and village
names TBD).* **Aenor burned them both down hunting her** — the same
pattern of kingdom-destruction Aenor later used on Caerwyn (the
heroine's home, §2.7). **Wren was the only survivor.** A child or young
teen when her village went under, she wandered alone for years, carrying
no people and no home.

Eventually she walked into the **Thornwood** uninvited. She had nothing
left to lose; she had no one to belong to. She did not ask Elian's
permission to enter his treeline.

She is the **second Weaver Elian buried** at the two-stones clearing
beneath the rowan (see §6.5). The first was Veyra (§2.2).

Elian told her on the first night what she was — believing that
foreknowledge would let him be quick enough this time. It did not. Wren's
body already knew how to flee (she had fled once before, from her
village), and the warning sent her running. **Aenor took her in five
years instead of ten** — accelerated because the warning made Wren
visible. Knowing what she was made her flee. Fleeing made her catchable.
The kingdom took her at thirty.

She *"tried to save everyone she met"* — possibly the wound shape of a
survivor who had failed to save anyone in her own home. Her bond-magic
ran her dry.

This is the central shape of Elian's second wound: **knowledge made it
worse, not better.** He has fewer illusions than most about
love-as-protection. He believes warning the person you love is sometimes
how you kill them.

**Wren is the heroine's mirror.** Same kingdom-destruction by Aenor.
Same sole-survivor fate. The difference: the heroine had a Duke father, a
Duchess mother, the Siren, the High Mage, and The First Weave to send her
through the rift. **Wren had no one.** A late-game scene where the
heroine learns Wren's name and story is a major emotional beat to plant
(future arc §10.2).

### 2.5 The Fifth Weaver

**Status: fled the centring. Fate unknown.**

The Fifth saw what had happened to the Four before her and fled the kingdom.
She did not return. Her fate is unknown. Aenor's hunger has continued
regardless.

### 2.6 Proto · the Sixth Weaver

**Status: presumed consumed by Aenor. Actually survived. In hiding inside
the wardwork.**

**Proto is the exception.** He is the rare Weaver **born within Aethermoor's
borders** — the case the kingdom should not have produced (see §1.3). The
mystery of how he was born in Aethermoor at all is an open canon slot (see
§8.7). A **wardwright** by trade — one who worked on the kingdom's wards
before Aenor came for him.

Proto **fought Aenor when she came for him** ~150 years ago. He was young,
mortal-bodied. The fight was not survivable in the conventional sense. He
held her off long enough to lose himself **into the system** — the
spell-architecture of the kingdom's wards. Aenor believed she had finished
him. **She caught him sideways** — the consumption was *incomplete*, which
is the reason for her decay (see §3.5).

He has been **hiding inside the wards for roughly a hundred and fifty
years.** Born long after Veyra was already gone; he and Corvin/Noir never
overlapped in mortal life.

This is why Aenor isn't currently hunting Proto — **she thinks she ate him.**
He is, in his own words, *"a wound she stopped checking on."*

He has carried the five Weavers before him within his own consciousness —
the remnants Aenor consumed left echoes in the spell-system, and Proto
holds them. They speak to him. They speak through him. They cheer for the
player.

When the player meets Proto (Chapter 21+), Proto becomes **visible** for the
first time in a century and a half. **A clock starts ticking.** Aenor will
eventually realize. This is a future plot hook (see §10).

### 2.7 The Player · the Seventh Weaver

**Status: alive. Currently in the kingdom. Born in Caerwyn.**

The player is the daughter of a Duke of **Caerwyn** (§1.1). She is a true
seventh Weaver, born outside Aethermoor as the Weaver-from-outside rule
requires (§1.3). At fourteen, when Aenor came hunting her and Caerwyn was
burning, her parents — with the help of the Siren (Lyra's mother) and the
High Mage (Lucien's father) — cast forbidden magic to tear a rift and send
her through with **The First Weave**. She landed in the modern world.
Memory lost. She grew up there. Two years before Ch1 she was pulled back
through the rift. (Full origin, see `docs/story/STORY-BIBLE.md §4`.)

The player's defining trait: **they bond easily.** They can love many. They
can form bonds across all seven characters, across both kingdoms, across
mortal and supernatural. This is not a flaw. **It is the gift the kingdom
needs.**

Each affection tier-up the player achieves with a character is, mechanically
in the lore, **healing one wound** in the bond-fabric of the world.

## 3 · AENOR · THE ANTAGONIST

### 3.1 Who she is

Queen of Aethermoor. Caspian's paternal grandmother. Currently active and
ruling (de facto if not de jure). **Six hundred years old.** She has not
publicly announced her age; the kingdom assumes she is mortal. She is not.

**Aenor is queer.** Her preference has always been for women. In a kingdom
that required heirs and bound queens to political marriage, she could not
love openly. Both Selene (§2.1) and Veyra (§2.2) — the two Weavers her arc
turns on — were women she could have loved, in another life. She destroyed
both of them. **She has spent six centuries destroying the women she could
have loved.**

**She was not always queen.** Aenor is the *younger sister* of **AELRIC**,
the original heir of Aethermoor (see §7). She became queen only after
Aelric stepped aside in shame after the Selene tragedy (§2.1).

### 3.2 What she believes

Aenor is **not a simple villain.** She believes:

- Bond-magic causes vulnerability.
- Vulnerability causes weakness.
- Weakness causes war and suffering.
- Therefore Weavers — who weave bonds — are the source of chaos in the world.
- Therefore consuming them is **mercy** to the kingdom: she is removing the
  variable that destabilises everything.
- Therefore she is the kingdom's **true protector.**

She believes this with absolute conviction. She is wrong. But she is
genuinely convinced. This is what makes her a tragic villain rather than a
flat one.

### 3.3 What she actually is

A grieving, jealous immortal who learned by accident that consuming Weavers
extends her life. The chain of cause:

1. **~650 years ago — Selene.** Aenor's brother Aelric attempted to kill
   her out of jealousy over Selene's love. Selene saved Aenor's life.
   Aenor, untrained and panicked, **accidentally consumed Selene's bond-
   light.** She was horrified. She burned every record of Selene's name,
   kept Selene's ring, and **vowed never again.** Aelric stepped aside;
   Aenor became Queen.
2. **~600 years ago — Veyra.** Cassien (a Duke with royal blood) brought
   Veyra to Aethermoor to marry her under the Weaver-Marriage Law and
   overthrow Aenor. Aenor met Veyra and tried to convince her to *"join
   hands"* — the offer was queer-coded; Aenor wanted Veyra as her
   Weaver-partner. Veyra refused; she wanted Corvin. Corvin came for her.
   **Aenor and Corvin fought a magical duel.** To win, Aenor **broke her
   vow and consumed Veyra's bond-light on the spot.** She gained Veyra's
   power. She sealed Corvin. **Cassien — calculating — proposed an
   alliance with Aenor afterward; she accepted.**
3. The Veyra consumption restored Aenor's youth past her natural span and
   gave her the power-rush she had been resisting for fifty years. *This
   time she liked it.*
4. Aenor convinced herself this was justified — Weavers were dangerous,
   she was the kingdom's true protector (see §3.2).
5. She has consumed every Weaver Aethermoor encountered since — the Third,
   the Fourth, the Fifth — and a botched attempt on the Sixth, Proto (see
   §3.5).
6. **Weavers are rare.** Aethermoor imports them under the Weaver-Marriage
   Law (§1.4); the import pipeline has dried up. Aenor has gone hungry for
   decades between meals. The hunger is part of what made her cruel.

**Aenor is NOT a Weaver.** She had only the diluted royal bond-magic in her
youth. Everything she has, she stole. Each consumption tops up a power that
is not hers and was never hers. This is the structural contrast with the
player (the true Seventh Weaver, who **bonds** rather than **eats**).

### 3.4 Where she rules from

The palace at Aethermoor's centre. She has not walked the palace in a
hundred years. She is announced via courier and decree. The Dowager
Notices is a Chapter 26 beat where she begins paying attention again.

Chapter 27 is the chapter where she walks the palace for the first time
in a century, **arriving for the player.** The arc that follows is open
(per owner direction: main story stays serial, no finale).

### 3.5 The Proto Mis-Consumption · Aenor's Power Decay

**~150 years ago.** Aenor came for Proto the way she had come for every
Weaver since Veyra. Proto fought. He held her off long enough to lose
himself into the wardwork. Aenor **thought** she had eaten him whole. She
hadn't.

The mis-consumption left a **slow bleed in her magic** — the first time in
450 years she had absorbed an incomplete Weaver. For decades, she did not
notice. The decay was glacial.

**~80 years ago — Aenor's panic.** For the first time in centuries she felt
age in her hands. Her hair greyed at the temples; she pulled it out. Her
teeth went sensitive to cold. She did the math. Without a Weaver to consume,
she was running out.

She made two decisions in that decade. One: she would consume the next
Weaver that emerged, no matter who. Two: she needed an heir — for the first
time since taking the throne — in case the decay outpaced the next meal.

### 3.6 The Constructed Heir · how Aurelius was made

Aenor had no living natural family. Her brother **Aelric** had abdicated
six centuries earlier and faded from records (§8.1). Her husband **Cassien**
(§8.5) had been dead five centuries — their political alliance had never
produced a natural child. She had buried him in the royal vault beneath the
cathedral with full state honors that were politically obligatory but
personally meaningless.

**~80 years ago, on a night with no witnesses, Aenor went down to the
vault.** She **dug up Cassien's grave with her own hands.** She took from
his bones what she needed — the femur, a rib, the skull-cap, three teeth —
and carried them back to a sealed chamber in the east wing. There she wove
them into a working using:

- Cassien's bones (the bloodline anchor — his cadet-branch royal blood
  carried legitimacy).
- Her own essence (the maternal seed).
- Bond-fragments she had consumed from minor Weavers and never finished
  digesting (the life-fuel).

A child was conceived in that chamber. He was born ten months later, in
the same chamber, with no witnesses. **He was fully mortal once born** —
but his existence was **necromantic in origin.** The kingdom would never
know. The cathedral floor was relaid before dawn.

This is **AURELIUS — Caspian's father.** Aenor named him, raised him,
trained him. She was disappointed by him; he turned out kind. He had
Cassien's quiet eyes. (See §6.3 for the Caspian-line continuation.)

### 3.7 Selene's Ring

Aenor wears a thin silver band on the little finger of her right hand.
It is six hundred and fifty years old. It was given to her by Selene at
sixteen, before either of them understood what they were to each other.
Aenor has never taken it off — not while sleeping, not while bathing, not
while consuming Weavers, not while digging up her dead husband's bones.

**No one currently alive knows what the ring is.** Caspian thinks it is
a coronation token. Lucien suspects it is older than coronation but has
not pressed. The ring is **Aenor's one tell** — she touches it when she
is grieving and does not know she is grieving. When the player notices,
the player will be the first.

## 4 · DARK MAGIC

### 4.1 What it is

**Dark magic is The Deep, unwoven.** Specifically: the wounds left in the
bond-fabric where Weavers were consumed. Each consumed Weaver left a place
where the raw underground magic of The Deep (§1.2) is no longer being
woven into bonds. It leaks up. It corrupts. It births wound-creatures.

**Visual canon (locked in Ch1, recurs Ch6, all wound-creature deaths):**
when a creature born from a wound is killed, the dark magic leaks out as
black smoke — **not soot-black, absence-black** — in slow ribbons pulling
toward the dark places between the trees. The body left behind goes
ash-pale and falls wrong, decaying as if it had died years ago and only
just been allowed to know it. The smoke is the wound's gravity finding its
way home. Any character with the eyes to see it (Weavers, the seven LIs at
high affection, Proto) recognizes the smoke for what it is. Ordinary people
see only "a wolf, the wrong shape."

Where dark magic appears in the world:
- **The Thornwood** is rotting at its eastern edge — a wound at the
  two-stones clearing beneath the rowan, growing toward the graves of
  Veyra and the Fourth Weaver (see §6.5). The wound has been growing for
  sixty years; Elian has been holding it closed alone.
- **Corvin's seal** was steeped in dark magic — Aenor's hunger leaked into
  the binding. It has now corroded (see §6.6).
- **The northern shore caves** (Lyra's home) hum with old voices — Veyra's
  people fled through there.
- **The palace east wing** holds an absence (where the Third Weaver sealed
  her daughter; the daughter's magic is gone but the door remembers).
- **The fallen town past the headland** — a town that has already been
  consumed by leaked Deep-magic, now bones. *(A walkable warning of what
  awaits Aethermoor if the seventh Weaver fails.)*

### 4.2 Why it's spreading

Six wounds for six centuries. The bond-fabric of the world is
**undermended.** The wounds are pulling at the seams. The kingdom is
slowly coming apart, structurally and magically.

**This is accelerating because the Weaver-import pipeline has collapsed**
(see §1.5). With no Weavers entering Aethermoor, The Deep is no longer
being woven at all — not just *missing repair* but *no new weaving.*

Players see this in:
- The Fading meter (Kingdom health) — visible in-game
- Idle dialogue from each character noticing strangeness
- Chapter 13 (Aenor's arrival) confirming the threat is named

### 4.3 What heals it

**Bonds.** Specifically: bonds woven by the seventh Weaver across multiple
characters. Each affection tier-up restitches a piece of the fabric. Each
crossover (when two of the player's bonded characters meet) reinforces the
weave.

The player's path is **not** to defeat Aenor in combat. The path is to
**weave so densely** that Aenor's consumption can no longer take more than
the world replaces. When the bonds outpace her hunger, she starves.

This makes **the romance the plot.** Not parallel to it. The same thing.

## 5 · WHAT MAKES THE WORLD BREAK

The world breaks when the bond-fabric thins below sustainability. Symptoms:

- Magic stops flowing through wards. Lucien's tower notices first.
- The Thornwood begins rotting from the inside.
- The siren caves echo without singers.
- The dark prince's seal weakened — but in the wrong direction (more dark
  came through, not less). *(This is what eventually broke it; see §6.6.)*
- The kingdom's people begin distrusting each other reflexively.
- Buildings become structurally unsound (the bonds of trust that hold
  builders' work fail).

If the seventh Weaver fails — is consumed, or refuses to bond, or chooses
the dark path — the world unbinds.

If the seventh Weaver succeeds — bonds densely, across multiple characters,
toward genuine love — the world re-stitches.

If the seventh Weaver chooses the **dark-leaning path** of selective
consumption (see §10.5) — she may save herself and rebuild her own kingdom
(Caerwyn) at the cost of becoming a corrupted Weaver and walking the line
that ended in Aenor.

## 6 · THE SEVEN CHARACTERS

Each character has a **canonical role in the lore** plus their romance arc.
The romance and the lore role are designed to reinforce each other.

### 6.1 ALISTAIR · Captain of the Guard

**Lore role:** the **embodiment of duty as bond.** Alistair has been a knight
for twenty years. His oath is the bond between a man and the kingdom he
swore to. His arc is the **transformation of duty into love** — when his
oath becomes about a person rather than an abstract realm.

He is common-born. He has lived under Aenor's reign his entire career. He
will eventually need to choose between loyalty to the crown and loyalty to
the player. This choice is **already encoded** in his turning-point (`go` /
`stay`) and his three endings.

He has never met Aenor face-to-face. He has only seen her decrees.

### 6.2 LYRA · Voice of the Caves

**Lore role:** descendant of a **massacred siren-people.** Her mother's
people were hunted by Aenor's forces (siren-voices were considered
chaos-bringers). Her mother died in the persecution. Lyra escaped at
fifteen, fled to the northern caves, and has been the only siren left
since.

Her voice carries the **bond-song** her mother taught her. The song is
ancient bond-magic. It is what is keeping the caves themselves from
collapsing into the sea.

She is half-sister to **Lucien** (see §6.4). Their shared father —
identity TBD (see §8.4) — is connected to the Aethermoor royal line.

**Her mother — the Siren — helped cast the rift on THE NIGHT** (~150 years
ago) that sent the heroine to safety. Lyra's mother died later in the
siren persecution. (See `docs/story/STORY-BIBLE.md §2 THE NIGHT`.)

### 6.3 CASPIAN · Crown Prince

**Lore role:** **the heir in the queen's shadow.** He is the grandson of
Aenor. He has been raised in her palace. He has watched her not visibly
age. He has, since boyhood, suspected she was not what she claimed to be.

His arc is the transformation of charm-as-armor into honest-love. The
hidden subplot beneath this is **his slow realization that his
grandmother is the source of the rot.**

His turning-point (name the player publicly, or keep her hidden) is also a
political choice: naming her invites Aenor's notice. Hiding her is
strategic but cowardly.

#### 6.3.1 Caspian's lineage (full canon)

The Aethermoor royal line, post-Aelric, is **not natural.** It is a
constructed line. This is the chain, ordered from oldest to present:

- **AELRIC** *(Aenor's older brother).* Original heir. Stepped aside in
  shame after the Selene tragedy ~650 years ago (see §2.1, §8.1). His fate
  after the abdication is an open canon slot.
- **AENOR** *(Aenor became queen by ascension after Aelric stepped aside).*
- **CASSIEN** *(Caspian's grandfather, §8.5).* From a cadet branch of the
  royal house — descended from the younger brother of an earlier king.
  Royal blood close enough for the Weaver-Marriage throne-claim, far
  enough that marriage to Aenor was not incestuous. **Brought Veyra to
  Aethermoor to overthrow Aenor; lost her in the duel; allied with Aenor
  afterward.** Shared her bed politically; no natural child. Died ~550
  years ago. **His grave was emptied by Aenor herself ~80 years ago** for
  his bones (see §3.6).
- **AURELIUS** *(Caspian's father).* Born ~70 years ago. **Magic-woven
  from Cassien's bones, Aenor's essence, and consumed bond-fragments**
  (see §3.6). Fully mortal once born, but necromantic in origin. Aenor
  raised him. He turned out kind — Aenor was disappointed. He had
  Cassien's quiet eyes.
- **LIRIEN** *(Caspian's mother).* Aethermoor minor nobility. Quiet,
  observant, devout. Loved Aurelius from before the court arranged their
  marriage. Knew early that Aenor was wrong. Kept her mouth shut to
  protect her children.
- **CASPIAN** *(current crown prince, ~25 years old).* Aurelius insisted
  on the name — a soft mutation of **Cassien**, the grandfather Aurelius
  did not know was the Duke who tried to overthrow Aenor. **Aenor
  permitted the name.** She does not know how to react to it.
- **MIRA** *(Caspian's younger sister, dead at six).* Bright, restless,
  asked too many questions for a princess. Killed when the family was
  caught trying to flee the kingdom (see §6.3.2). Her bedroom in the east
  wing has not been touched in fifteen years. Caspian visits it on the
  anniversary.
- **THE HIDDEN BROTHER** *(name TBD; currently ~5 years old).* Lirien's
  secret third child, born at her family seat, told to court as a
  stillbirth. **Given to Lirien's old nurse and hidden in a coastal
  village far from the capital.** Neither Caspian nor Aenor knows he
  survived. Future-arc reveal slot.

#### 6.3.2 The Bridge — how Caspian's parents died

**~15 years ago.** Aurelius and Lirien suspected what Aenor was. They
decided they could not keep their living children near her any longer.
One night they took Caspian (10) and Mira (4) and rode north toward the
Nocthera border, hoping to cross and disappear.

**Aenor sent her own riders.** Aurelius and Lirien were killed at the
bridge. Mira was killed when she would not stop crying. Caspian was the
only survivor — pulled from the water by a guard, returned to the palace,
raised by his grandmother. The hidden baby was never mentioned because
Aenor did not know to look for him.

**The official story.** A carriage went off the bridge in the dark. The
family drowned. Only Caspian was pulled out alive. The kingdom mourned.

**What Caspian remembers.** Being lifted out of a wet carriage by a
guard. Nothing before that. He has not believed Aenor's flood-story since
he was sixteen. He has never said this aloud. **The player is the first
person he will tell.**

### 6.4 LUCIEN · Scholar of the Tower

**Lore role:** the **scholar who knows everything except his own feelings.**

This is the **revised character architecture** (May 2026). Lucien is a
grand-master mage. Magic does not cost him anything material — not memory,
not life, not time. The reason is darker:

**Lucien is, for most of his life, hollow of human emotion.**

He does not feel love. He does not feel hate. He does not feel envy or
jealousy. He has been told this since he was eight (the age at which he
realized other children felt things he did not). He decided, with the
detachment of a scholar, that emotion was inefficient. He chose **logic**
as his ground.

He cares for the tower of magic out of **duty, not love.** He prefers
books to people. He refuses to take part in conflict. He acts as a
**medium** — an information broker — because his neutrality is his
strongest defense and his most useful trait to others.

He knows everything in the kingdom's archives. He knows nothing about
what he himself feels.

**The player's arrival is the first time the system fails.** He starts
reacting to her in ways his equations cannot describe. He tries to
catalogue the reactions. He cannot. The catalogue grows. He realizes he
has been **wrong about emotion's inefficiency for thirty years.**

His magic does not cost him. Loving her costs him **everything he
believed about himself.**

His lore obligations:
- His half-sister Lyra (see §6.2) is the **first person whose existence
  he ever felt anything about.** He has been hiding the family register
  page for years, telling himself the feeling was annoyance. The Weaver
  forces him to name it.
- His scorched pages about Nocthera are **how the player learns Corvin
  was a real person, not a ghost story.**
- His tower is structurally one of the few buildings in the kingdom
  still bond-strong, because he has been **maintaining the wards for
  forty years out of duty.**
- **His father — the High Mage — helped cast the rift on THE NIGHT** that
  sent the heroine to safety (~150 years ago). Lucien does not yet know
  the connection.
- **He holds The First Weave** (the master grimoire of Weaver magic) in
  his tower, confiscated as a dangerous artifact. He has been studying
  it for years and cannot fully read it — it is Weaver-magic, he is a
  mage.

His dark route: he **chooses the cold again.** Closes the system.
*"The variable named you is cleared."* This is his Buffer Overflow
equivalent.

### 6.5 ELIAN · Warden of Thornwood

**Lore role:** **the man who buries grief.** Elian is older than he
looks — his mother was something older than human. He has watched the
kingdom be four kingdoms. He has not aged in centuries.

**The two stones beneath the rowan.** In a clearing by the creek, off
the path north of his hut, **two** old weather-worn stones sit side by
side in the moss. The grass between them is greener than the grass
around them. A rowan tree stands above them. Elian has tended this
clearing alone for centuries. Aethermoor erased both graves from
records. Elian preserved them.

- **First stone: Veyra** (the Second Weaver, §2.2). **She was Elian's
  first love.** When they were both young, she would slip from Nocthera
  into the Thornwood to meet him in secret. Their love was true but
  forbidden — she was Nocthera royalty, he was a wood-warden; the love
  could not be made public. Many years later, when Cassien brought Veyra
  to Aethermoor and Aenor consumed her in the duel with Corvin
  (§2.2), **Elian felt Veyra's consumption via the forest-whisper.** He
  followed it. He found her body where it had fallen. *No one else had
  come for her.* He carried her to the rowan and buried her here because
  she had loved this part of the wood.
- **Second stone: WREN** (the Fourth Weaver, §2.4). Many years later,
  in his isolation period after Veyra. He had stopped letting anyone
  past his treeline. **Wren walked through anyway** — she had nothing
  left to lose; Aenor had burned her village and her kingdom to find
  her, and she was the only survivor. She did not ask his permission.
  He told her on the first night what she was, believing the warning
  would let him be quick enough this time. **The kingdom took her in
  five years instead of ten — knowing made it worse, not better.**
  Wren's body already knew how to flee — she had fled once before,
  from her village.

**Elian has buried two of the women he loved.** Veyra (first love,
teenager, forbidden, secret in the wood) and the Fourth (second love,
years later, his isolation period). This is the **defining loss** that
shapes his entire arc.

**These two stones are distinct from the Wardens Interlude site.** The
Wardens hill (separate location, eight stones, his predecessors as
Warden of Thornwood) is his **POST** grief. The two stones beneath
the rowan are his **HEART** grief. Both scenes coexist in canon;
do not merge them.

**The new layer (May 2026 owner direction): fear.**

Elian is afraid:
- That he will fail the player the way he failed his two Weavers.
- That **dark magic is consuming the Thornwood** — there is a black
  place at the two-stones clearing, growing under the rowan toward
  the graves themselves. It has been growing for sixty years. He
  has not told anyone.
- That the world is rotting beyond his ability to hold it.
- That **warning the player makes it worse, not better.** This is
  the wound shape of the Fourth Weaver's death. He believes
  foreknowledge accelerates the kingdom's hunger. This is why he
  has been slow to tell the player what she is — the warning
  itself is what catches a Weaver's scent.

His fear is the motor that drives his arc. His turning-point (carve
or leave) is **a fear-management choice** — does he let the player
into the grief, or does he keep the grief private to protect them.

His dark route (rotting forest) is the moment his fear overwhelms
his discipline. The trees rot. The dark magic claims him.

His lore connection to a future arc: the **Forest dark-magic crisis**
(see §10) is his crisis. The player saves the Thornwood beside him.
The black place is growing toward the graves of the two women he
buried — **Aenor's leakage threatens his memorial directly.**

### 6.6 NOIR · Prince Corvin Noctalis · the Freed Prince

**Lore role:** **the man Aenor sealed for loving Veyra — now free, hunting
her in return.**

Corvin Noctalis was the heir of Nocthera. He met Veyra during the political
negotiations for her marriage. They loved each other. Veyra chose him over
Cassien (and over Aenor). When Cassien brought Veyra to Aethermoor to
overthrow Aenor under the Weaver-Marriage Law (§1.4), Corvin came after her
to protect her.

**Aenor and Corvin fought a magical duel.** Catastrophic. To win, Aenor
consumed Veyra's bond-light on the spot — gained her power — and **sealed
Corvin** with the expanded power that gave her. (See §2.2.)

**Where she sealed him** is still an open canon slot (see §8.3). What is
canonical about the seal:

- The seal was **not a tomb.** Corvin was alive in some form.
- The seal was **not visible** to most of the kingdom — Aenor erased it.
- The seal was **steeped in dark magic** — Aenor's hunger leaked into the
  binding from the moment she cast it.

**The seal has now broken.** It rotted over six centuries; Aenor's decay
from mis-consuming Proto (§3.5) corroded it sharply across 150 years; the
heroine's arrival as a living Weaver, ~2 years before Ch1, resonated the
last of it apart. **Noir has walked free for roughly two years.** He
hides in fallen Nocthera and slips into Aethermoor to gather intel,
locate Aenor, and plan **revenge.**

Six centuries of restraint have changed Corvin. He has stopped using his
real name. He has only been "Noir" for so long.

**The fall of Nocthera came one generation after Corvin was sealed.** The
kingdom collapsed without its heir. The bond-fabric of Nocthera died. The
land became bone.

When Corvin's care arc reaches deep enough, the player can take him to
**ruined Nocthera through the seam** (a residual thin place where the seal
once was). The land has been seen breathing again — soft, but real. (See
"Nocthera, Quietly" aftermath scene and "Corvin Restored" ending.)

His arc fork: **revenge, or a future.** A careless kill of Aenor releases
every wound her hunger has been containing — it would destroy the world
Veyra died loving. Veyra's memory and the heroine's bond pull him from
*raze* toward *rebuild.*

#### 6.6.1 The burnt page the heroine carries (canon, May 2026)

The burnt page the heroine carries from Ch1 is a **seal-page torn from
The First Weave** — the master grimoire of Weaver magic, which holds
(among everything else) part of Noir's recorded binding. Tearing that
page loose detonated six centuries of bound magic, flung her to the south
Thornwood, and took her memory (her second amnesia).

Full canon — The First Weave, THE NIGHT, the heroine's origin — lives in
`docs/story/STORY-BIBLE.md`.

### 6.7 PROTO · the Sixth Weaver in Hiding (the Exception)

See §2.6. His character is his lore.

The rule: Weavers are not born in Aethermoor (§1.3). **Proto is the
exception** — the rare Weaver born within Aethermoor's borders, ~150 years
ago. His existence was a quiet anomaly the mage tradition kept secret;
Aenor came for him before his home-grown Weaver-status could destabilize
the import-marriage politics. *Why* Aethermoor produced him at all is an
open canon question (see §8.7).

His care arc is the **player slowly making him visible** — pulling him
out of the wards, giving him a body (in the manifest ending), letting
him be seen for the first time in a century and a half.

He carries the five Weavers before him. They speak through him. They
are the player's allies in any future confrontation with Aenor — they
have been waiting a long time for this.

**Early-presence canon (Ch1–Ch8 spine, locked in May 2026):** Proto is
reachable in the kingdom as a **seam-whisper** from Day 3 of the
player's arrival onward (Ch4 of the spine). He cannot manifest a form
yet — the wards are too thin and Aenor's gaze would catch him — but
he can speak through silver-thread voice in liminal places (corridor
shadows behind tapestries, mirror seams, the half-light between
torches).

His earliest voiced appearance (Ch4 of the spine):
> *"hi. sorry. i know this is weird. i can't appear yet. the wards
> aren't stable enough. a friend. that's all i can prove right now."*

Golden-Retriever warm. Lowercase. The only character besides Lyra
who reaches the player in her "home" register from first contact.

**Stakes when she reaches him face-to-face (Ch20):** the player is the
first person to look back at him in a century and a half. The moment she
sees him, Aenor's pattern-recognition starts to fire. The seam-whisper
from Ch4-onward was already a risk; meeting him in person starts the
endgame clock.

## 7 · CONTINUITY RULES (must not violate)

These are the rules every writer must follow. Violations create the kind

1. **Selene is the First Weaver.** Brought to marry Aelric (Aenor's
   brother); died saving Aenor from her brother's attempted fratricide;
   accidentally consumed.
2. **Veyra is the Second Weaver.** Not the first. Not the third. Elian's

   and Corvin** — not over a decade.
3. **Corvin/Noir was sealed in the same duel where Veyra was consumed.**
   The sealing and the consumption happened in the same magical event.
4. **Noir walked free ~2 years before Ch1.** He is no longer actively
   sealed.
5. **Aenor is 600+ years old.** Caspian is her grandson. The age gap is
   real and supernatural.
6. **Aenor is queer.** Her romantic interest is in women. Her marriage
   to Cassien was a political alliance, not love.
7. **Aenor is the younger sister of Aelric** (the original heir who
   stepped aside).
8. **Proto is the Sixth Weaver.** Player is the Seventh.
9. **Proto is the exception to the Weavers-from-outside rule** — born in
   Aethermoor. Why is a mystery slot.
10. **Weavers are not born in Aethermoor** (except Proto). They are
    imported from other kingdoms via the Weaver-Marriage Law.
11. **Marrying a Weaver = legal throne-claim in Aethermoor.**
12. **Aenor consumed (or attempted to consume) all six prior Weavers.**
    Proto survived but Aenor doesn't know.
13. **Nocthera fell one generation after Corvin was sealed.** Not at the
    time of his sealing.
14. **Lucien has no human emotion until the player arrives.** Magic does
    not cost him memories. It costs him nothing material.
15. **Lucien's half-sister is Lyra.** Their shared father is connected

16. **Elian buried Veyra at the rowan in the Thornwood.** The grave is
    hidden but not lost. **Veyra was his first love** (forbidden,
    teenager, secret in the wood).
17. **Dark magic is The Deep unwoven.** Specifically, the wounds left
    where Weavers were consumed. Not a separate force.
18. **The kingdom is the bonds Weavers wove from The Deep.** It breaks
    when bonds thin. It heals when bonds thicken.
19. **Aenor believes she is protecting the kingdom.** She is not evil
    in her own framing. Writers should never make her cackle.
20. **Caerwyn was the heroine's birth kingdom.** Destroyed by Aenor
    ~150 years ago. Now ruins.

### 8.5 CASSIEN · Caspian's grandfather

**Status: dead ~550 years ago. Bones disturbed by Aenor ~80 years ago.
Caspian was named after him without knowing the full truth.**
*(Cosmology-lock rewrite, May 2026.)*

Cassien was a Duke of Aethermoor from a **cadet branch of the royal
house** — descended from the younger brother of an earlier king. He had
royal blood close enough to mount a throne-claim under the
Weaver-Marriage Law (§1.4), but far enough that marriage to Aenor was
not incestuous.

**Cassien brought Veyra to Aethermoor** for the purpose of marrying her
and overthrowing Aenor. His motive was political, not romantic — Veyra
was the vehicle to the throne, not the woman he loved. When Veyra
refused him (she wanted Corvin), Cassien stayed his hand and waited.

When Corvin came to Aethermoor and **Aenor consumed Veyra on the spot in
the magical duel** with Corvin (§2.2), Cassien's plan collapsed. He had
lost his Weaver-pawn. He had watched Aenor grow into the strongest piece
on the board.

**He calculated. He proposed an alliance.** He would marry Aenor, bring
his cadet-branch royal legitimacy and his military strength, and reign
at her side. **Aenor accepted.** They shared a bed because the alliance
required it. **They never had a natural child** — Aenor's queer
preference; possibly her newly-stolen bond-magic made her infertile;
the marriage was political duty, not love. Cassien held his Dukedom and
his queen's-consort title until he died ~550 years ago. Officially:
"a long illness." Truthfully: ambiguous. Grief, age, possibly Aenor's
quiet poison.

**Buried in the royal vault beneath the cathedral** with full state
honors. **~80 years ago, Aenor opened his grave.** With her own hands.
She took from his bones what she needed for the working that made
Aurelius (see §3.6). The femur. A rib. The skull-cap. Three teeth. The
cathedral floor was relaid before dawn. No one alive knows this happened.
Caspian visits the vault on his grandfather's death-day every year. The
coffin still appears intact. **It is empty of the pieces that mattered.**

**Cassien's old household** survives as a quiet underground in the
kingdom. The retainers who served him in life — and their children, and
their children's children — have continued to watch the court. **They
are the most likely source of the unsigned elder letters in Ch4** (see
§8.7). Their loyalty is to a man who tried to take the throne and failed,
and who allied with the queen rather than dying. *(Their position toward
Aenor is therefore complicated: they served his alliance with her, but
they remember he had tried to overthrow her first. A faction that could
turn against her if a new Weaver-marriage opportunity emerged.)*

**Caspian was named after Cassien.** Aurelius insisted on the name without
fully understanding why he insisted. (Magic-woven children sometimes
inherit affinities from the bones they were made from.) **Aenor permitted
the name.** She does not know how to react to it. When she says
*"Caspian"* aloud, she is — without admitting it to herself — saying
*"Cassien"* a half-step softer.

## 9 · THE WEAVER (player) ROLE

The player is the **last hinge** in the bond-fabric. Their role is not
combat. It is **bonding densely enough to outpace Aenor's consumption.**

Mechanically:
- Each affection tier-up = one wound restitched.
- Each crossover unlocked = one cross-bond reinforced.
- Each ending earned = one character anchored permanently.

The player is not "saving" any single character. They are **saving the
world by loving the people in it.**

The dark route: the player consumes instead of bonds. They become the next
Aenor. The world's last hinge becomes its last hunger.

The bittersweet route: the player bonds with one but not enough to anchor
the world. The kingdom holds for now. The threat returns in the next
generation.

The good route: the player bonds with multiple, the world re-stitches,
Aenor starves of her supply.

**The dark-leaning route — see §10.5** *("The Weaver-Queen Returns")* —
the player consumes selectively to take back Caerwyn. She becomes a
**corrupted Weaver** but reclaims her birthright. She does not become Aenor,
but she walks closer to that line than the good route allows.

threatens Elian's memorial directly:** the dark is reaching for the
love-keeping ground he has tended alone for centuries.

The player + Elian + (Lyra's bond-song?) work to heal the wound.
Elian must face that he has been holding the rot back single-handedly
for sixty years AND that his fear of failure is now also his training
— he knows the wound's pattern better than anyone living. He has been
**reading the rot in private** the whole time. This arc is where he
stops reading alone.

This is the player's **first taste** of fighting dark magic directly.

Foreshadowing seeds (already planted in Ch1–8 spine):
- Ch1: dark-smoke-from-a-wound-creature visual establishes the
  absence-black aesthetic.
- Ch6: a second wound-creature kills earlier in the season than the
  spawn-rate suggests. Elian's *"Two of you in one season. I am tired
  of you."* names the acceleration aloud for the first time.
- Ch7: Elian's *"deer with no reflection / something has started
  calling at night"* worldbuilding line, foreshadowing wider rot.
- Ch8: the greener grass between the two stones — the love-keeping
  ground is **resisting** the rot. That resistance is the key the
  player needs to learn to weaponize at scale. Bond-tending heals
  bond-wounds, but only on ground that has been tended.

### 10.3 The Cataloged Voice (Lucien × Lyra rift)

Lucien's research — the wards, the catalog of bond-mechanics — could
let Aenor's heirs cage sirens again. Lyra refuses to be a research
subject. Their shared father resurfaces. Lucien must choose between
duty and his sister.

This arc is **a sibling rift, not romantic rivalry.** They are
half-siblings.

### 10.4 The Endgame

Aenor walks the palace for the player. Chapter 27's cliffhanger pays
off in this arc. The player's seven bonds become the weapon.

This is the **major arc closer** — not the game closer. New arcs can
follow. Owner direction (May 2026): main story has no final ending.
The world continues.

### 10.5 The Weaver-Queen Returns (Caerwyn rebuilt)

**The dark-leaning endgame for the heroine.** *(Cosmology-lock pass, May
2026.)*

The Light Route saves Aethermoor and Nocthera; the heroine anchors to
her chosen LI(s); her own birth kingdom **Caerwyn** (§1.1) stays in
ruins — the sacrifice of home for the world.

**The Dark-Leaning Route — "The Weaver-Queen Returns"** — the heroine
**consumes selectively.** She adopts some of Aenor's methods to defeat
Aenor. She **returns to Caerwyn** and rebuilds it from ashes, reigning
there as Weaver-Queen. She becomes a **corrupted Weaver** — someone
harder, scarred, *not the same Weaver she could have been.* The line
between her and Aenor narrows. Caerwyn breathes again; her soul has a
shadow on it.

**Foreshadowing seeds to plant in mid-game (Ch31+):**
- The page in her sleeve is from her *home kingdom* — when she
  remembers, she remembers what she lost.
- Lucien's bone-magic page hints that consumption is a *technique*,
  not an inherent evil — a Weaver could use it for *protection.*
- Noir's revenge arc parallels hers — *"would you have done what I
  did?"*
- The heroine starts wanting *more* — not just survival, restoration.
- The corrupted-Weaver path becomes available when she first willingly
  consumes (even something small).

This is the route's moral fork: **save the world purely, or take back
what was taken — and lose part of yourself doing it.**
