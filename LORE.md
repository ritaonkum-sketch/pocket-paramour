# LORE.md — Pocket Paramour Canonical Bible

This is the **single source of truth** for all worldbuilding, character history,
and lore. Any narrative edit (chapter, tier scene, letter, crossover, ending,
care-route line) must conform to what is written here.

If a fact below contradicts something in code, the LORE.md version is correct
and the code is the bug. If a fact is missing from LORE.md, it is **not yet
canonical** and the writer should propose it here before authoring it into the
game.

Last revised: May 2026.

## 1 · THE WORLD

### 1.1 Two kingdoms

The story takes place across two kingdoms whose histories are entangled:

- **Aethermoor** — the surviving kingdom. Ruled (de facto for six centuries) by
  Queen Aenor. Aenor's grandson is Crown Prince Caspian, current heir.
- **Nocthera** — the **fallen** kingdom. Once a peer of Aethermoor across the
  northern sea. Collapsed roughly 600 years ago, one generation after its heir
  was sealed away. The land is bone now. (The land has been seen breathing again
  in Noir's deepest care route — see §6.6.)

The two kingdoms were rival peers before the events that broke Nocthera. They
were never enemies in war; they were rivals in trade, in succession claims, and
in marriage politics.

### 1.2 The bond-magic that holds the world together

Magic in Aethermoor is **bond-magic** — the kingdom's structural integrity is
literally woven from the trust, love, and ties between its people. Streets
exist because someone trusted a builder. Walls stand because someone protected
a city. Magic flows because someone loved someone.

When bonds are strong, the kingdom is whole. When bonds thin, the world thins.

The people who weave bond-magic are called **Soul Weavers**, or just Weavers.

## 2 · THE WEAVERS (1–7)

There have been seven Soul Weavers in the kingdom's history. Aenor's reign
overlaps with all of them.

### 2.1 The First Weaver

**Status: name lost to time. Older than even Proto remembers.**

What is known: there was a first Weaver before Aenor's reign even began. Her
name is gone. Aenor encountered her early. The fate is unknown but she did not
survive. The First is a deliberate **mystery slot** — future writers may reveal
who she was.

### 2.2 Veyra · the Second Weaver

**Status: consumed by Aenor over a decade, ~600 years ago.**

A minor royal of **Nocthera**. Originally promised in political marriage to
**Aenor** (then a young queen of Aethermoor) to seal peace between the two
kingdoms.

Veyra met **Caspian's grandfather** during the betrothal negotiations. The two
fell in love. Veyra also met **Prince Corvin Noctalis** (now known as Noir).
Both princes loved her.

She **chose Corvin.** Aenor's marriage was annulled by Veyra's refusal.

What followed:
- Aenor **sealed Corvin** for taking what she had been promised. (See §3.1 for
  the where/how of his sealing — currently TBD, see §10.)
- Aenor began consuming Veyra. The consumption took roughly a decade.
- During that decade, Veyra fled north with Caspian's grandfather.
- Caspian's grandfather did not know how to save her. He could only grieve.
- **Elian** (the Thornwood warden, see §6.5) buried Veyra's body at the rowan
  tree in his forest after she was found. Aethermoor erased the grave from
  records. Elian preserved it.
- Veyra's last words were heard by **Proto** (the sixth Weaver, see §2.6) — he
  carries them.

Proto's recollection (Chapter 25): *"Aenor had been consuming her for a decade.
She was already dying when I met her."*

### 2.3 The Third Weaver

**Status: sealed her own daughter, went mad.**

Per Proto's midnight monologue (`affection-scenes.js`): the Third Weaver tried
to protect her own daughter from Aenor by sealing the daughter's magic away.
The act broke the Third Weaver's mind. Whether she was consumed by Aenor or
died of her own madness is unspecified.

### 2.4 The Fourth Weaver

**Status: tried to carry everyone, burned out at thirty.**

The Fourth attempted to save everyone she met. Her bond-magic ran her dry. She
died of exhaustion at thirty. Aenor's consumption of her happened post-mortem
or was unnecessary; canonical answer is open.

### 2.5 The Fifth Weaver

**Status: ran.**

The Fifth saw what had happened to the Four before her and fled the kingdom.
She did not return. Her fate is unknown. Aenor's hunger has continued
regardless.

### 2.6 Proto · the Sixth Weaver

**Status: presumed consumed by Aenor. Actually survived. In hiding.**

This is the **revised lore** (May 2026). Earlier framing said Proto "got stuck
sideways" passively in the seal. The canonical version is more active:

Proto **fought Aenor when she came for him.** He was a young Weaver of his
generation, mortal-bodied. The fight was not survivable in the conventional
sense. He held her off long enough to lose himself **into the system** — the
spell-architecture of the kingdom's wards. Aenor believed she had finished
him. He has been **hiding inside the wards for six hundred years.** She has
not been looking.

This is why Aenor isn't currently hunting Proto — she thinks she ate him.
He is, in his own words, *"a wound she stopped checking on."*

He has carried the five Weavers before him within his own consciousness — the
remnants Aenor consumed left echoes in the spell-system, and Proto holds
them. They speak to him. They speak through him. They cheer for the player.

When the player meets Proto (Chapter 21+), Proto becomes **visible** for the
first time in six centuries. **A clock starts ticking.** Aenor will eventually
realize. This is a future plot hook (see §10).

### 2.7 The Player · the Seventh Weaver

**Status: alive. Currently in the kingdom.**

The player arrived through a portal with no memory. Aethermoor's bond-magic
reached across worlds and pulled them in. They are the **last chance** to
restitch the world before Aenor's consumption pattern collapses everything
that remains.

The player's defining trait: **they bond easily.** They can love many. They
can form bonds across all seven characters, across both kingdoms, across
mortal and supernatural. This is not a flaw. **It is the gift the kingdom
needs.**

Each affection tier-up the player achieves with a character is, mechanically
in the lore, **healing one wound** in the bond-fabric of the world.

## 3 · AENOR · THE ANTAGONIST

### 3.1 Who she is

Queen of Aethermoor. Caspian's paternal grandmother. Currently active and
ruling (de facto if not de jure). **Six hundred years old.** She has not
publicly announced her age; the kingdom assumes she is mortal. She is not.

### 3.2 What she believes

Aenor is **not a simple villain.** She believes:

- Bond-magic causes vulnerability.
- Vulnerability causes weakness.
- Weakness causes war and suffering.
- Therefore Weavers — who weave bonds — are the source of chaos in the world.
- Therefore consuming them is **mercy** to the kingdom: she is removing the
  variable that destabilises everything.
- Therefore she is the kingdom's **true protector.**

She believes this with absolute conviction. She is wrong. But she is
genuinely convinced. This is what makes her a tragic villain rather than a
flat one.

### 3.3 What she actually is

A grieving, jealous immortal who learned by accident that consuming Weavers
extends her life. The chain of cause:

1. Aenor was promised Veyra in political marriage.
2. Veyra chose Corvin.
3. Aenor, humiliated and heartbroken, consumed Veyra in retaliation.
4. The consumption **kept Aenor alive past her natural span.**
5. Aenor convinced herself this was justified — Weavers were dangerous.
6. She has consumed every Weaver since, claiming purity. Each one bought her
   another century.

**Aenor was, herself, the First Weaver who could not love.** This is the
canonical explanation for why she had no soul-mate to weave with. Her
inability to bond has corroded into her inability to *let bonds exist* in
the world she rules.

### 3.4 Where she rules from

The palace at Aethermoor's centre. She has not walked the palace in a
hundred years. She is announced via courier and decree. The Dowager
Notices is a Chapter 26 beat where she begins paying attention again.

Chapter 27 is the chapter where she walks the palace for the first time
in a century, **arriving for the player.** The arc that follows is open
(per owner direction: main story stays serial, no finale).

## 4 · DARK MAGIC

### 4.1 What it is

**Dark magic is the wounds of consumed Weavers.**

Every Weaver Aenor consumed left a wound in the bond-fabric of the world.
Six wounds, one per generation. The wounds did not heal. They spread.

Where dark magic appears in the world:
- **The Thornwood** is rotting at its eastern edge — a wound opened when
  Veyra was buried there (her grave is the rowan).
- **Corvin's seal** is steeped in dark magic — Aenor's hunger leaked into
  the binding.
- **The northern shore caves** (Lyra's home) hum with old voices — Veyra's
  people fled through there.
- **The palace east wing** holds an absence (where the Third Weaver sealed
  her daughter; the daughter's magic is gone but the door remembers).

### 4.2 Why it's spreading

Six wounds for six centuries. The bond-fabric of the world is
**undermended.** The wounds are pulling at the seams. The kingdom is
slowly coming apart, structurally and magically.

Players see this in:
- The Fading meter (Kingdom health) — visible in-game
- Idle dialogue from each character noticing strangeness
- Chapter 13 (Aenor's arrival) confirming the threat is named

### 4.3 What heals it

**Bonds.** Specifically: bonds woven by the seventh Weaver across multiple
characters. Each affection tier-up restitches a piece of the fabric. Each
crossover (when two of the player's bonded characters meet) reinforces the
weave.

The player's path is **not** to defeat Aenor in combat. The path is to
**weave so densely** that Aenor's consumption can no longer take more than
the world replaces. When the bonds outpace her hunger, she starves.

This makes **the romance the plot.** Not parallel to it. The same thing.

## 5 · WHAT MAKES THE WORLD BREAK

The world breaks when the bond-fabric thins below sustainability. Symptoms:

- Magic stops flowing through wards. Lucien's tower notices first.
- The Thornwood begins rotting from the inside.
- The siren caves echo without singers.
- The dark prince's seal weakens — but in the wrong direction (more dark
  comes through, not less).
- The kingdom's people begin distrusting each other reflexively.
- Buildings become structurally unsound (the bonds of trust that hold
  builders' work fail).

If the seventh Weaver fails — is consumed, or refuses to bond, or chooses
the dark path — the world unbinds.

If the seventh Weaver succeeds — bonds densely, across multiple characters,
toward genuine love — the world re-stitches.

## 6 · THE SEVEN CHARACTERS

Each character has a **canonical role in the lore** plus their romance arc.
The romance and the lore role are designed to reinforce each other.

### 6.1 ALISTAIR · Captain of the Guard

**Lore role:** the **embodiment of duty as bond.** Alistair has been a knight
for twenty years. His oath is the bond between a man and the kingdom he
swore to. His arc is the **transformation of duty into love** — when his
oath becomes about a person rather than an abstract realm.

He is common-born. He has lived under Aenor's reign his entire career. He
will eventually need to choose between loyalty to the crown and loyalty to
the player. This choice is **already encoded** in his turning-point (`go` /
`stay`) and his three endings.

He has never met Aenor face-to-face. He has only seen her decrees.

### 6.2 LYRA · Voice of the Caves

**Lore role:** descendant of a **massacred siren-people.** Her mother's
people were hunted by Aenor's forces (siren-voices were considered
chaos-bringers). Her mother died in the persecution. Lyra escaped at
fifteen, fled to the northern caves, and has been the only siren left
since.

Her voice carries the **bond-song** her mother taught her. The song is
ancient bond-magic. It is what is keeping the caves themselves from
collapsing into the sea.

She is half-sister to **Lucien** (see §6.4). Their shared father —
identity TBD (see §10) — is connected to the Aethermoor royal line.

### 6.3 CASPIAN · Crown Prince

**Lore role:** **the heir in the queen's shadow.** He is the grandson of
Aenor. He has been raised in her palace. He has watched her not visibly
age. He has, since boyhood, suspected she was not what she claimed to be.

His arc is the transformation of charm-as-armor into honest-love. The
hidden subplot beneath this is **his slow realization that his
grandmother is the source of the rot.**

His turning-point (name the player publicly, or keep her hidden) is also a
political choice: naming her invites Aenor's notice. Hiding her is
strategic but cowardly.

### 6.4 LUCIEN · Scholar of the Tower

**Lore role:** the **scholar who knows everything except his own feelings.**

This is the **revised character architecture** (May 2026). Lucien is a
grand-master mage. Magic does not cost him anything material — not memory,
not life, not time. The reason is darker:

**Lucien is, for most of his life, hollow of human emotion.**

He does not feel love. He does not feel hate. He does not feel envy or
jealousy. He has been told this since he was eight (the age at which he
realized other children felt things he did not). He decided, with the
detachment of a scholar, that emotion was inefficient. He chose **logic**
as his ground.

He cares for the tower of magic out of **duty, not love.** He prefers
books to people. He refuses to take part in conflict. He acts as a
**medium** — an information broker — because his neutrality is his
strongest defense and his most useful trait to others.

He knows everything in the kingdom's archives. He knows nothing about
what he himself feels.

**The player's arrival is the first time the system fails.** He starts
reacting to her in ways his equations cannot describe. He tries to
catalogue the reactions. He cannot. The catalogue grows. He realizes he
has been **wrong about emotion's inefficiency for thirty years.**

His magic does not cost him. Loving her costs him **everything he
believed about himself.**

His lore obligations:
- His half-sister Lyra (see §6.2) is the **first person whose existence
  he ever felt anything about.** He has been hiding the family register
  page for years, telling himself the feeling was annoyance. The Weaver
  forces him to name it.
- His scorched pages about Nocthera are **how the player learns Corvin
  was a real person, not a ghost story.**
- His tower is structurally one of the few buildings in the kingdom
  still bond-strong, because he has been **maintaining the wards for
  forty years out of duty.**

His dark route: he **chooses the cold again.** Closes the system.
*"The variable named you is cleared."* This is his Buffer Overflow
equivalent.

### 6.5 ELIAN · Warden of Thornwood

**Lore role:** **the man who buries grief.** Elian is older than he
looks — his mother was something older than human. He has watched the
kingdom be four kingdoms. He has not aged in centuries.

He buried **Veyra** (the second Weaver, see §2.2) in the rowan tree in
his Thornwood. He has tended the grave alone for six hundred years.
Aethermoor erased the grave from records. Elian preserved it.

This is his **defining loss.**

**The new layer (May 2026 owner direction): fear.**

Elian is afraid:
- That he will fail the player the way he failed Veyra.
- That **dark magic is consuming the Thornwood** — there is a black
  place under the rowan that has been growing for sixty years. He
  has not told anyone.
- That the world is rotting beyond his ability to hold it.

His fear is the motor that drives his arc. His turning-point (carve
or leave) is **a fear-management choice** — does he let the player
into the grief, or does he keep the grief private to protect them.

His dark route (rotting forest) is the moment his fear overwhelms
his discipline. The trees rot. The dark magic claims him.

His lore connection to a future arc: the **Forest dark-magic crisis**
(see §10) is his crisis. The player saves the Thornwood beside him.

### 6.6 NOIR · Prince Corvin Noctalis · the Sealed Prince

**Lore role:** **the man Aenor sealed for loving Veyra.**

Corvin Noctalis was the heir of Nocthera. He met Veyra during the
political negotiations for her marriage to Aenor. They loved each
other. Veyra chose him.

Aenor sealed him roughly 600 years ago. **The where and how of his
sealing is currently TBD — see §10.** What is canonical:

- The seal is **not a tomb.** Corvin is alive in some form.
- The seal is **not visible** to most of the kingdom — Aenor erased it.
- Corvin can reach across the seal in limited ways — through the
  **seam** (a thin place in reality he opens with effort).
- The seam has been growing thinner over the past decade. The player's
  bonds may be the cause.
- Six centuries of restraint have changed Corvin. He has stopped using
  his real name. He has only been "Noir" for so long.
- He carries a **fragment** he can give to the Weaver — once given, she
  carries a piece of him. He can feel her through it. (See his "Bonded"
  ending.)

The fall of Nocthera came **one generation after Corvin was sealed.**
The kingdom collapsed without its heir. The bond-fabric of Nocthera
died. The land became bone.

When Corvin's care arc reaches deep enough, the player can take him to
**ruined Nocthera through the seam.** The land has been seen breathing
again — soft, but real. (See "Nocthera, Quietly" aftermath scene and
"Corvin Restored" ending.)

### 6.7 PROTO · the Sixth Weaver in Hiding

See §2.6. His character is his lore.

His care arc is the **player slowly making him visible** — pulling him
out of the wards, giving him a body (in the manifest ending), letting
him be seen for the first time in six centuries.

He carries the five Weavers before him. They speak through him. They
are the player's allies in any future confrontation with Aenor — they
have been waiting six centuries for this.

## 7 · CONTINUITY RULES (must not violate)

These are the rules every writer must follow. Violations create the kind
of contradiction that breaks the story for the player.

1. **Veyra is the Second Weaver.** Not the first. Not the third.
2. **Corvin/Noir was sealed for loving Veyra.** Not for any other reason.
3. **Aenor consumed Veyra over a decade.** Not in a single act.
4. **Aenor is 600+ years old.** Caspian is her grandson. The age gap is
   real and supernatural.
5. **Proto is the Sixth Weaver.** Player is the Seventh.
6. **Aenor consumed (or attempted to consume) all six prior Weavers.**
   Proto survived but Aenor doesn't know.
7. **Nocthera fell one generation after Corvin was sealed.** Not at the
   time of his sealing.
8. **Lucien has no human emotion until the player arrives.** Magic does
   not cost him memories. It costs him nothing material.
9. **Lucien's half-sister is Lyra.** Their shared father is connected
   to the Aethermoor royal line (TBD details).
10. **Elian buried Veyra at the rowan in the Thornwood.** The grave is
    hidden but not lost.
11. **Dark magic is the wounds of consumed Weavers.** Not a separate
    force, not Aenor's spell. The wounds.
12. **The kingdom is the bonds Weavers wove.** It breaks when bonds
    thin. It heals when bonds thicken.
13. **Aenor believes she is protecting the kingdom.** She is not evil
    in her own framing. Writers should never make her cackle.

### 8.3 Corvin's seal — WHERE IS HE
**This is the most important open question.** Owner direction (May 2026):
*"we might add detail later that how and where exactly Noir got lock away
and where his whereabouts is hiding."*

Proposals to choose from when the time comes:
- **Beneath the palace.** A dungeon under Aethermoor's east wing. Aenor

## 9 · THE WEAVER (player) ROLE

The player is the **last hinge** in the bond-fabric. Their role is not
combat. It is **bonding densely enough to outpace Aenor's consumption.**

Mechanically:
- Each affection tier-up = one wound restitched.
- Each crossover unlocked = one cross-bond reinforced.
- Each ending earned = one character anchored permanently.

The player is not "saving" any single character. They are **saving the
world by loving the people in it.**

The dark route: the player consumes instead of bonds. They become the next
Aenor. The world's last hinge becomes its last hunger.

The bittersweet route: the player bonds with one but not enough to anchor
the world. The kingdom holds for now. The threat returns in the next
generation.

The good route: the player bonds with multiple, the world re-stitches,
Aenor starves of her supply.

## 10 · FUTURE ARCS (set up by current lore)

These arcs are designed in concept but **not yet authored.** Each is
strongly supported by the lore above. Order is recommended; final order
is owner's call.

### 10.1 The Two Crowns (Caspian × Noir)

When Noir's "Corvin Restored" ending fires, Nocthera is breathing. In a
future arc, the rebuilt Nocthera and Aenor's Aethermoor come into
political tension. Caspian — Aenor's grandson — must choose between
his queen-grandmother and the prince his line wronged. The player is
the bridge.

Foreshadowing seeds to plant: Nocthera envoys at court, reactions to
*"that woman's land breathing again."*

### 10.2 The Forest That Was Never Just Trees (Elian × Dark Magic)

Elian's fear (now planted in his arc) becomes the trigger. The black
place under the rowan grows. The Thornwood begins rotting from the
inside. The player + Elian + (Lyra's bond-song?) work to heal the wound.

This is the player's **first taste** of fighting dark magic directly.

Foreshadowing seeds: dialogue about deer fleeing, branches blackening,
Elian receiving warnings he does not share.

### 10.3 The Cataloged Voice (Lucien × Lyra rift)

Lucien's research — the wards, the catalog of bond-mechanics — could
let Aenor's heirs cage sirens again. Lyra refuses to be a research
subject. Their shared father resurfaces. Lucien must choose between
duty and his sister.

This arc is **a sibling rift, not romantic rivalry.** They are
half-siblings.

### 10.4 The Endgame

Aenor walks the palace for the player. Chapter 27's cliffhanger pays
off in this arc. The player's seven bonds become the weapon.

This is the **major arc closer** — not the game closer. New arcs can
follow. Owner direction (May 2026): main story has no final ending.
The world continues.
