---

## What this document is

This is the **operational brand foundation** for Pocket Paramour. It is the document the team consults when deciding whether ANY design choice — a button shape, a font weight, a particle color, a chapter title, a music swell, a UI copy line — fits the brand or not.

Every section here gives **decision criteria**, not vague mood. The format is consistently:

> *"X looks like this. X does NOT look like this. Test: would the brand approve?"*

Use this doc as a yes/no lens. If a proposed design fails three or more sections of this document, it doesn't ship.

---

## SECTION 1 — Brand essence

### The one-sentence promise

> **Pocket Paramour is the Otome where loving someone gently is how you save the world.**

That's the entire game. Care is not a side mechanic. Care IS the plot. The romance is not a reward you unlock; the romance is the unlocking.

### The three-word distillation

> **Tender · Mythic · Inevitable**

- **Tender** — every action the player takes is an act of care. Even cosmic stakes are reached through small intimate gestures.
- **Mythic** — the stakes are world-scale, the lore is deep, the characters are old and burdened, the bonds being woven hold a kingdom up.
- **Inevitable** — the romance feels like recognition, not pursuit. Like the player and the character were always going to find each other. The unfolding has weight.

### The longer descriptive paragraph

Pocket Paramour is a regal-romantasy Otome × Tamagotchi hybrid where the player, the Seventh Weaver of a dying kingdom, restores the bond-fabric of the world by caring densely for seven men across slow-burn romances. The visual language is illuminated manuscript meets Korean manhwa court-elegance meets fae-court romantasy. The writing is literary fiction quality. The pacing is page-turn graceful. The promise is that loving someone with full attention is the most powerful act in the universe — and the game makes that literal.

## SECTION 2 — Brand values

Five operational principles. Every design decision is tested against these.

### Value 1 — **Tenderness over spectacle**

We earn emotion through small intimate gestures, not loud production. A character placing his hand over hers carries more weight than a 3-second particle explosion.

- **Looks like**: a single beat that holds for 3 seconds while two people sit in silence
- **Doesn't look like**: lens-flare on every triumph, slow-motion zooms on every reveal, screen-shake for emphasis
- **Test**: would a Korean manhwa artist draw this beat with a half-page panel of two people not speaking? If yes, brand-aligned.

### Value 2 — **Slow is sacred**

Mobile Otome usually rushes because the genre assumes attention is limited. We assume the opposite. Players who choose Pocket Paramour are choosing slow. We honor that.

- **Looks like**: 400-700ms cubic-bezier transitions, multi-second holds on important beats, page-turn rhythm
- **Doesn't look like**: instant cuts between beats, auto-advance text, snappy zippy micro-animations
- **Test**: does the rhythm of this scene feel like reading a book, or like swiping a feed? Book = brand-aligned.

### Value 3 — **Literary respect**

Players are treated as readers, not consumers. The writing carries weight. We don't dumb down, we don't shorthand, we don't fill empty silence with chatter.

- **Looks like**: 50-beat chapters with restraint, dialogue that earns its meaning, narration that trusts the reader to feel without being told what to feel
- **Doesn't look like**: tutorial-text everywhere, emoji-driven character chat, manga-style stylized "shocked!" reactions
- **Test**: would an editor at Tor Books or a Korean webtoon Top-100 publication be embarrassed by this writing? If yes, rewrite.

### Value 4 — **Manuscript over machine**

The interface should feel like an artifact of the world, not a layer on top of it. Every UI surface is a page, a letter, a torn parchment, a manuscript margin. The interface IS the lore.

- **Looks like**: parchment backgrounds, ink-quill typography, gold filigree corner flourishes, candle-warm lighting
- **Doesn't look like**: glass-card material design, neon-edge sci-fi panels, generic mobile-game chips
- **Test**: would this UI surface feel out of place in a fae-court manuscript? If yes, redesign.

### Value 5 — **The seven are sub-brands**

Pocket Paramour is the parent brand. Each of the seven characters is a sub-brand with its own color signature, motif, music, and emotional promise. When you're on Alistair's screen, the air feels different than when you're on Lucien's. The system honors that.

- **Looks like**: per-character palette tinting, per-character motif corner-marks, per-character ambient particle color
- **Doesn't look like**: identical UI for all characters with just a different portrait swapped in
- **Test**: blind the player to character name — could they tell which character's screen they're on from the color, motif, and music alone? If yes, sub-brands are working.

## SECTION 3 — Brand voice & tone

### How we sound

Pocket Paramour speaks like a contemporary literary fantasy novel. Specifically: late-2010s/2020s romantasy at the Sarah J. Maas / Holly Black / Rebecca Yarros tier, with Korean webtoon court-romance pacing layered in.

We are NOT pulp Otome that pretends to be literature. We are literature that happens to be Otome.

### Voice principles (locked from prior canon, restated here for completeness)

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Zero em-dashes** in player-facing text | Em-dash overuse is a tell of pulp / AI / corporate writing. Period, comma, ellipsis only. |
| **Smart curly apostrophes** (') everywhere | JS single-quoted string safety AND typographic professionalism |
| **No triadic rhythm crutches** | "She felt fear, sorrow, and shame." Cheap. Vary clause length. |
| **No negation-affirmation rhythm overuse** | "Her face did not move. Her eyes did." A character signature for Aenor only — leaks into other POVs are tells. |
| **No self-explaining emotion** | "She felt sad" is amateur. Show the body. The reader knows. |
| **British spelling** (armour, colour, parlour) | Matches the established canon and the manuscript / fae-court feel |
| **Italic stage directions inline** via `*(modifier)*` | Manhwa-script discipline carried into prose |
| **Generous narrative restraint** | Trust the reader. Withhold. The reader fills in. |

### Tone modes (mood-adaptive — same brand voice, different register)

| Mode | When used | Example texture |
|---|---|---|
| **Tender** | Care interactions, affection moments, intimate dialogue | warm cadence, gentle observation, long pauses |
| **Ceremonial** | Prologue, chapter openings, ritual beats | mythic register, weighted vocabulary, slow rhythm |
| **Threat** | Aenor scenes, wound-creatures, danger reveals | clipped sentences, sharp metaphor, restrained menace |
| **Grief** | Graves, losses, confessions | quietest register, single-clause sentences, silence as content |
| **Wit** | Banter (Alistair flirt, court repartee) | dry undercut, gentle teasing, never sarcastic |

### Anti-voice (we never sound like)

- Tutorial copy: "Tap here to feed Alistair!" (replace with: "He hasn't eaten today.")
- Marketing copy: "Unlock all 7 characters!" (replace with: nothing — let discovery be discovery)
- Generic Otome trope: "He gazed deeply into her eyes." (replace with body, weight, specificity)
- Corporate friendly: "Welcome back! Here are today's rewards." (replace with: a beat of in-world acknowledgment)

## SECTION 4 — Brand personality

### If Pocket Paramour were a person

A woman in her late twenties who reads romantasy in hardcover and dog-ears the page on first chapters. She wears a cream linen dress with gold thread embroidery. Her favorite color is the lavender of dawn before the sun is up. She journals in a leather-bound notebook with deckle edges. She lights a candle when she reads. She has a habit of underlining sentences twice when they pierce her. She is patient. She believes in slow love. She is not sentimental — she is *attentive*.

### Big-Five-style trait profile

| Trait | Where on the scale |
|---|---|
| **Openness** | Very high. Mythic, literary, dreamy, layered. Loves complexity and depth. |
| **Conscientiousness** | High. Polish matters. Every detail is intentional. |
| **Extraversion** | Low-medium. Inward, reflective, careful. Speaks softly but everyone listens. |
| **Agreeableness** | High but with edges. Tender but never naive. Will set a boundary in silk. |
| **Neuroticism** | Low. Calm under pressure. Grief without panic. |

### What she is NOT

- Not a perky cheer-bot. Pocket Paramour is not Animal Crossing.
- Not a dark edgelord. Pocket Paramour is not Bloodborne.
- Not a flirty manic-pixie. Pocket Paramour is not a Magic Mike montage.
- Not a corporate brand. Pocket Paramour does not say "Hey there!"

## SECTION 5 — Mood board (the locked references)

These are the canonical visual / emotional anchors. Every art, UI, and motion decision should evoke at least one of these.

### Anchor 1 — Korean manhwa court elegance (soft)
**Reference**: *Secret Lady, The Fantasie of a Stepmother, As the Heart Leads*
**Evokes**: candlelit ballrooms, soft-pastel gowns, gold thread embroidery, dawn through tall windows, ivory and lavender, refined feminine power, court intrigue rendered as quiet calculation.

### Anchor 2 — Korean manhwa court intrigue (dark)
**Reference**: *The Villainess is a Marionette, Beatrice*
**Evokes**: thrones at midnight, blood-wine drapery, sharp gold detail on black, candle-guttering reveals, dangerous beauty, the villainess in profile catching light, the kiss before the betrayal.

### Anchor 3 — Romantasy fae court (jewel-toned)
**Reference**: *A Court of Thorns and Roses (Maas), Quicksilver (Hart), Gild (Kennedy)*
**Evokes**: deep sapphire, antique gold, fae-prince predatory charm, opulent throne rooms, jewel tones used architecturally, the romance as dangerous worship.

### Anchor 4 — Sharp gothic fae (cold)
**Reference**: *The Cruel Prince (Black)*
**Evokes**: silver and bone, blade-shine, court politics as deadly sport, restraint in ornament, the fae who would kill you politely.

### Anchor 5 — Manuscript / leather-and-ink
**Reference**: *Fourth Wing (Yarros)* + the broader romantasy interior-design tradition
**Evokes**: hand-drawn maps, deckle-edged paper, drop caps, leather covers, gold-tooled spines, the book as a beloved object. The world as something you READ.

### Anchor 6 — Candle-mythic-feminine
**Reference**: *Bewitched, Court of Thorns and Roses (witch passages)*
**Evokes**: herbs hung from beams, salt circles, single candles in dark rooms, threads and charms, the feminine mystic power rendered with reverence not flash.

### Anchor 7 — The seventh-light (our own)
**Reference**: Pocket Paramour canon, Ch32 first conscious Weaver act
**Evokes**: cyan-toward-lavender glow rising under a hand, the bond-light warm and slow, ancient magic that looks like a candle being lit from inside someone's skin.

## SECTION 6 — Brand promises to the player

### What we promise

1. **You will feel seen.** The characters notice what you do. They remember. They change in response to your care. The relationship is not scripted past you — it is shaped by you.
2. **You will read writing that respects you.** No tutorials yelling at you. No emoji-driven character chat. No filler. Every beat is there because it needs to be.
3. **Your tenderness will matter.** Small gestures (feed, wash, talk, gift) carry cosmic weight. Saving the world is what you're doing when you remember to wash someone's hair.
4. **You will feel the weight of your choices.** The Light vs Dark route is not a button at the end. It is the accumulating shape of every care decision you've made. The ending feels earned.
5. **You will feel beauty.** Every screen is designed. Every transition is choreographed. Every font is chosen. The game will be one you screenshot.

### What we deliberately do NOT promise

- We do not promise fast progression. (Slow is the point.)
- We do not promise gacha thrills. (Care is the loop, not pulls.)
- We do not promise edgy darkness for its own sake. (Tenderness over spectacle.)
- We do not promise constant comedy. (We can be witty in moments; we are not a sitcom.)
- We do not promise voice acting at launch. (When budget allows; not gated on it.)

## SECTION 7 — Sensory language

How the game *feels* if it were physical objects. Used to guide art direction, music selection, particle behavior, sound design.

### Smells
Beeswax candles, old paper, vellum, woodsmoke from a banked fire, rosewater, ink, leather binding, autumn forest after rain.

### Sounds (ambient palette)
A page turning. A candle catching. A wood fire settling. A distant horn at dawn. A loom shuttle running. Silk against stone. The single chime of a small bell. A held breath.

### Textures (tactile palette)
Parchment grain. Linen weave. Velvet drape. Aged gold leaf. Polished bone. Cool stone. Worn leather. Silk thread. A candle-warmed glass globe.

### Light register (visual palette)
Candle warmth. Moonlight cool. Hearth glow. Dawn-mist diffusion. The single beam through a tall window. Lantern in a dark wood. Never overhead fluorescent. Never electric.

### Pace (rhythmic palette)
Page-turn. Silk-unfold. Candle-catch. Held breath. Bell-toll. Slow exhale. Wave at a quiet shore. A character looking up after a long silence.

## SECTION 8 — Per-character brand fragments

Each of the seven men is a sub-brand within Pocket Paramour. Plus the Heroine and Aenor as the two non-romanceable color identities. Each fragment locks:

- **Color signature** (the palette tint when they're on screen — from MARKET-STUDY.md Section 2.3)
- **Visual motif** (the shape/symbol/texture that belongs to them)
- **Voice signature** (how they speak)
- **Romance archetype** (the trope they embody, repurposed for our register)
- **Music register** (what their leitmotif feels like)

### 1 · Alistair — Captain of the Dawn Patrol

- **Color**: captain-gold, dawn-warm cream, polished steel
- **Motif**: gauntlet, sheathed sword, kitchen postern, the captain's healing draught
- **Voice**: *"Mi'lady"-er.* Restrained, formal, undercut by sudden tenderness. Apologizes by going still.
- **Archetype**: The quiet honorable knight who carries beautiful things in his bare hands.
- **Music**: brass restrained, single hum of held breath, dawn horns in distance

### 2 · Elian — Warden of the Thornwood

- **Color**: deep forest green, moss, antler-cream, woodsmoke
- **Motif**: the green cloak, the bow, the two stones at the creek, the wood's quiet
- **Voice**: wood-rough, dry, withholds the kinder version of the truth. Speaks rarely.
- **Archetype**: The six-century immortal who has buried his loves and is trying not to bury another.
- **Music**: low cello, single struck wood, the sound of a forest at dusk

### 3 · Lyra — Voice of the Eastern Caves

- **Color**: pearl, sea-silver, deep ocean blue, siren-shimmer
- **Motif**: the loom, the cave entrance, a half-song in the throat, bare wet feet
- **Voice**: clipped when needed, lyrical when allowed. The last of her line; carries the weight.
- **Archetype**: The siren who could sing the world apart but chooses to hide.
- **Music**: distant choir, single note held impossibly long, water dripping in a cave

### 4 · Caspian — Crown Prince

- **Color**: royal sapphire, palace-marble, princely silver-blue
- **Motif**: the desk, the unanswered correspondence, the sealed letter under the cup, the slate-grey study coat
- **Voice**: princely, charming widely, loves quietly. Witty undercut. Real grief beneath the polish.
- **Archetype**: The prince who appears to play but is playing chess with the queen.
- **Music**: piano restrained, single chord held, the silence after the laugh

### 5 · Lucien — Most Powerful Mage in Aethermoor

- **Color**: dark blue-violet, tower-amber, starlight gold
- **Motif**: the monocle, the long blue hair, the tower window, the print on the wards, the prepared scroll
- **Voice**: composed past human limit, polite-impossible in court, cracks at the eyes when alone.
- **Archetype**: The scholar who reads the world for what it costs and gives more than anyone notices.
- **Music**: single violin held, the chime of a small bell at distance, a candle catching

### 6 · Noir — The Sealed Prince (Corvin Noctalis)

- **Color**: black velvet, bone-white, sealed-silver, six-century shadow
- **Motif**: the seal, the dark crown, the cold throne of dead Nocthera, the shadow that hasn't seen sun in 600 years
- **Voice**: ancient, careful, every word weighed for a century before spoken.
- **Archetype**: The dark prince who watched the world end once and has been waiting to be allowed to want again.
- **Music**: low organ, distant bell tolling, the silence of a vault

### 7 · Proto — Anomaly in the Wards

- **Color**: static-cyan, mirror-silver, five-faces violet
- **Motif**: the mirror, the five faces sharing his mouth, the static-shimmer, the question "may I"
- **Voice**: quick, anxious-tender, asks permission for everything, the five voices sometimes leak through
- **Archetype**: The ghost-in-the-machine who is more afraid of the player than the player is of him.
- **Music**: glass harmonica, the shimmer of a tuning fork, soft static

### The Heroine — The Seventh Weaver

- **Color**: the seventh-light (cyan-toward-lavender), candle-warm, dawn-pink
- **Motif**: the torn page with the burnt seal, her bare hand, the bond-light rising under her palm
- **Voice**: second-person ("you"). The favorite of the rose. Court-trained restraint, modern-world iron underneath.
- **Archetype**: The reluctant savior who didn't ask for the gift but is using it anyway.
- **Music**: solo piano warm, the seventh-light shimmer, the breath before speaking

### Aenor — The Queen

- **Color**: wine-blood, royal aubergine, gilded black, dying-gold
- **Motif**: the bone-pale globe, the hair-pin with the violet stone, the hidden chamber, the consumed Weavers
- **Voice**: court-warm for show, very-quiet-to-herself in private. The eyes do what the smile does not.
- **Archetype**: The villainess who was once a woman in love.
- **Music**: low strings sustained, a single golden chord that turns wrong, the chime of a small instrument she's been holding for hundreds of years

## SECTION 9 — Brand decision matrix

When evaluating any design choice — UI element, particle effect, font weight, music cue, copy line, character pose — run it through this five-question test:

| # | Question | Pass if... |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does it serve **Tenderness over spectacle** (Value 1)? | Yes, it earns its emotion through specificity, not volume |
| 2 | Does it honor **Slow is sacred** (Value 2)? | Yes, the rhythm respects the player's attention |
| 3 | Does it pass **Literary respect** (Value 3)? | Yes, a literary editor would approve |
| 4 | Does it feel like **Manuscript over machine** (Value 4)? | Yes, it could be an artifact of the world |
| 5 | Does it strengthen a **sub-brand** (Value 5)? | Yes, it deepens at least one character's signature |

**Scoring:**
- **5/5 passes** — ship it
- **4/5 passes** — ship if the one missing pass is acceptable for the context (e.g., a Aenor scene that's deliberately NOT tender)
- **3/5 passes or fewer** — redesign

This matrix is the **practical guard** against the design drifting back toward generic mobile-Otome conventions. Use it.
